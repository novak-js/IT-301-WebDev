Build your PC, step by step

Take your time and keep your manuals nearby. This guide follows a basic air-cooled desktop build; your case or cooler may need a different order.

In this guide

  1. Get ready
  2. Prepare the motherboard
  3. Fit the CPU cooler
  4. Move into the case
  5. Finish the connections
  6. Start up your PC

Keep nearby

  • Suitable screwdrivers (I recommend avoiding power tools)
  • Component manuals
  • A container for screws

From components to OS

1. Get ready

  • Use a clean, well-lit work surface and organize your parts.
  • Keep the power supply switched off and unplugged during assembly.
  • Use appropriate anti-static precautions. Handle boards by their edges and avoid touching contacts or socket pins.

2. Prepare the motherboard

  • I highly recommend that you read the manual of your specific motherboard before proceeding; the procedure differs for RAM placement, M.2 mounting, and I/O pin orientation depending on the brand.
  • Follow the socket instructions to align and seat the CPU without forcing it into place.
  • Install RAM in the slots specified by the motherboard manual, matching the module's notch to the slot.
  • If using an M.2 SSD, fit it in a supported slot and secure it with the board's screw or retention clip.

3. Fit the CPU cooler

  • Install the mounting hardware specified for your socket.
  • Remove any protective film from the cooler's contact surface. Use pre-applied thermal paste, or apply paste according to the cooler instructions.
  • Secure the cooler evenly and connect its fan to the CPU fan header.

4. Move into the case

  • Position motherboard standoffs only beneath matching mounting holes.
  • Fit a separate rear I/O shield if needed, then mount the motherboard without overtightening its screws.
  • Install the power supply with its ventilation positioned as the case instructions specify.

5. Finish the connections

  • If using a graphics card, secure it in the recommended PCIe slot and attach its required power cables.
  • Mount any SATA drives and connect both data and power cables.
  • Connect motherboard power, CPU power, case buttons, USB, audio, and fans using the manuals. Use PSU-approved cables and keep them clear of fan blades.

6. Start up your PC

  • Check for loose screws and unfinished connections before connecting wall power.
  • Connect a keyboard and monitor. Use the graphics card's display output if one is installed.
  • Switch on the PSU and press the case power button. Check detected memory, storage, and CPU temperature in BIOS/UEFI, then install your operating system and updates.
  • If you're feeling adventurous (or you're just sick of Microsoft...) maybe give Tux a try?
