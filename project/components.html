Get to know your components

Every part has a job. Learn the basics before choosing the pieces for your purrfect PC.

CPU Motherboard Graphics Memory Storage Power supply Case Cooling

Processor (CPU)

The central processing unit runs program instructions. It handles work such as calculations, application tasks, and game logic.

What to check: Choose a processor for your intended workload. Its socket and model must be supported by the motherboard and its BIOS version.

Motherboard

The motherboard connects the processor, memory, storage, and expansion cards. It also provides connections for devices such as keyboards and USB drives.

What to check: Match the processor socket, memory type, and case size. Check that there are enough ports and expansion slots for your needs.

Graphics (GPU)

The graphics processing unit creates the images shown on your screen. It can also accelerate tasks in supported editing and creative applications. Some processors include integrated graphics; others need a separate graphics card.

What to check: Consider your games, applications, and monitor resolution. A separate card also needs enough case space and the correct power connections.

Memory (RAM)

RAM temporarily holds data that running programs are using. It provides working space for applications, but it does not replace the storage used for your files. This will unfortunately be the most expensive component of most builds due to:

  • Circular financing by AI companies
  • Long-term agreements for memory supply
  • Alleged price fixing by memory manufacturers

The end result is pricing so ridiculous that in the coming years it will likely bring an end to personal computing, self-hosting, and the very idea of owning your own hardware.

What to check: Choose a capacity suited to your applications and multitasking. The memory generation and capacity must be supported by your processor and motherboard.

Storage

Storage keeps your operating system, programs, and files when the computer is turned off. Solid-state drives provide fast access, while hard drives are another option for larger file collections.

What to check: Allow room for your files and applications. Check supported drive connections, M.2 drive sizes, and available drive bays.

Power supply (PSU)

The power supply converts electricity from the wall into the power used by the PC's components.

What to check: Match the complete system's power needs, connector requirements, and case space. For a modular power supply, use cables approved for that exact model.

Case

The case holds the parts in place and provides space for airflow, cooling, and cables. Common motherboard sizes include ATX, Micro-ATX, and Mini-ITX.

What to check: Confirm support for your motherboard size, graphics card length, CPU cooler height, and power supply dimensions.

Cooling

A CPU cooler carries heat away from the processor, while case fans move air through the case. Some processors include a cooler, while others require one separately.

What to check: Confirm the cooler's socket support, mounting hardware, and clearance. Check the case's fan or radiator mounting locations.
